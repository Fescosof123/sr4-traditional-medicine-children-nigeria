# 2. Strategy development, validation and peer review

The MEDLINE strategy was taken from the registered protocol and is built
from three concept blocks (traditional or herbal medicine use and care
seeking; children and caregivers; Nigeria with its states and major
cities) combined with AND. Controlled vocabulary (MeSH) and free-text
title and abstract terms are combined with OR within each block. No
design, date or language filter was applied; records indexed as
animal-only were excluded.

Validation against seed studies. Before the searches were run, nine
known eligible studies were identified by scoping searches and used to
test the registered MEDLINE strategy. All nine were retrieved: PMIDs
19113999, 19193235, 21860147, 22363898, 22640367, 23909090, 34607333,
36101691 and 39889503.

Peer review. The search author first checked the strategy against the
PRESS 2015 elements (McGowan et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2016;75:40-46) and
found no syntax errors. An independent check by a second reviewer
\[authors to name the reviewer\] identified synonyms missing from the
registered strategy: complementary medicine and complementary therapies,
phytotherapy and medicinal plants, folk and indigenous medicine,
traditional birth attendants, ethnopharmacology and ethnobotany,
traditional or alternative treatments and therapies, unorthodox
medicine, self-treatment, health care seeking and herbal remedies in the
first block; numeric under-5 forms, babies, toddlers, preschool
children, parents, guardians and schoolchildren in the second block; and
the cities Jos, Uyo, Makurdi, Nnewi, Sagamu, Ogbomoso, Ife, Lokoja and
Minna in the third. The reviewer also noted that the registered strategy
had no MeSH term for care seeking, whereas the Embase translation used
'help seeking behavior'/exp. These terms were added as supplementary
lines in MEDLINE and Embase, the two core bibliographic databases,
before the records were exported. In MEDLINE they added 211 records to
the 886 retrieved by the registered strategy (202 from the first block,
38 from the second and 8 from the third, with some overlap). The
regional and grey-literature sources (Europe PMC, Global Index Medicus,
AJOL, Google Scholar and OpenAlex) were searched with the registered
strategy only, which is recorded as a deviation (Section 5).

# 4. Record management and deduplication

All exports were saved as structured files, one per source, in
03_search/raw_records with the search date in the file name. Records
were merged (4,430 records) and duplicates removed with a scripted
procedure () in three exact stages and one fuzzy
title-similarity stage, followed by a manual review. Stage 1 matched
exact DOIs, stage 2 exact PubMed identifiers, and stage 3 exact
normalised titles (all hyphen and dash forms replaced by a space,
apostrophes and quotation marks deleted, Google Scholar format tags such
as \[PDF\] removed, HTML and diacritics removed, lower case, titles of
25 characters or fewer ignored). Exact-title matches were merged even
when the DOIs differed, which covers preprint and published versions,
conference abstracts later published as articles, errata and republished
versions; an exact-title match between records carrying two different
PMIDs was sent to manual review instead. Because conference supplements
and collections often share one DOI across many abstracts, an exact DOI
or PMID match was accepted automatically only when the two titles had a
similarity ratio of at least 0.50 (Python difflib SequenceMatcher on the
normalised titles); lower-similarity matches were sent to manual review.
Stage 4 then compared the titles of all remaining records pairwise (a
token-overlap prefilter with Jaccard index of at least 0.5, then the
similarity ratio) and flagged pairs with a similarity ratio of 0.90 or
more for manual review; pairs whose titles differed in length by 60
characters or more were not compared.

Automatic merges: 1,027 by DOI, 306 by PMID and 226 by title. Manual
review of 65 flagged pairs: 22 pairs sharing a supplement DOI were
different abstracts and were kept apart; one pair sharing a PMID with
different titles was version 1 and version 3 of the same F1000Research
article and was merged; two exact-title pairs with conflicting PMIDs
(one three-record cluster: a paper indexed twice in PubMed and once in
Europe PMC) were merged; of 40 near-title pairs, 31 were the same report
or the same study (case, punctuation, spelling or diacritic variants,
Google Scholar truncation, preprint and published versions, or a paper
indexed twice) and were merged, and 9 were kept apart (different papers
with similar titles, or conference abstract and article pairs whose
titles differed, to be linked at screening if they prove to be one
study). The 34 merge decisions produced 32 merges, because two pairs
were already in one cluster (the three records of one paper indexed
twice in PubMed and once in Europe PMC). A reason is recorded for every
pair in the deduplication log. Clusters were resolved with a
union-find structure so that the record kept for each cluster is the
final survivor; the log records, for each removed record, the cluster
record it matched, the two records actually compared, the stage, the
title similarity and the final kept record. The survivor is chosen by
source priority (PubMed, Embase, Europe PMC, Global Index Medicus,
OpenAlex, AJOL, Google Scholar) and, within a source, by the longer
abstract; missing fields of the survivor were filled from its
duplicates.

Result: 1,591 duplicates removed (1,559 automatic and 32 manual); 2,839
unique records carried forward to title and abstract screening (retained
source: Embase 1,165, PubMed 1,095, Google Scholar 177, AJOL 206,
OpenAlex 105, Global Index Medicus 50, Europe PMC 41). After the final
run, a full pairwise check of the retained titles found no pair with a
similarity ratio of 0.90 or more other than the 9 pairs deliberately
kept apart. Two PubMed records were removed as duplicates of other
PubMed records, each a paper indexed twice in PubMed under two PMIDs in
the same journal (one exact-title match and one near-title manual
merge). The merged file, the deduplicated file, the log and the manual
decisions are saved in 04_dedup.

# 5. Deviations from the registered protocol

\(a\) Supplementary search terms identified at peer review were added to
the MEDLINE and Embase strategies before export; the regional and
grey-literature sources were searched with the registered strategy only.
(b) AJOL accepted a nested Boolean string, so a full strategy was run
rather than the simplified strings anticipated, with the term reductions
stated in Section 3.5. (c) The Google Scholar string dropped the state
and city names and added a fourth block (prevalence OR use OR
utilization). (d) Grey literature from repositories and theses was
searched through the OpenAlex index rather than by visiting individual
university repositories, and the FMoHSW and NNMDA websites were not
hand-searched on the search date; this will be completed before
screening closes and reported with its date. (e) The MICS 1995 and 1999
reports were not located. (f) In Embase, the 26 clinical trial registry
records returned alongside the article records were not exported. (g)
Records were retrieved through application programming interfaces and a
web browser rather than a reference manager, and deduplication was done
with a scripted, logged procedure instead of reference-manager
deduplication.
